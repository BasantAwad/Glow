# ADR 0001: Initial stack and pilot constraints

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-08-24
- Decision owners: Glow founder/team
- Applies to: Phase 0 prototype baseline and initial pilot planning

## Context

Glow is an AI mental-health support and care-navigation product. It is not a human therapist, licensed clinician, diagnostic tool, emergency service, or replacement for professional care. The repository previously contained plans but no application stack, source code, dependencies, deployment configuration, or approved data providers.

The engineering constitution requires the initial stack to preserve a server-authoritative safety control plane, allow a provider-abstracted model gateway, support real-time modalities later, and avoid accepting sensitive conversations before applicable privacy, security, legal, clinical, and operational decisions are in place.

## Decision

Glow will use a monorepo containing:

- A Python 3.11+ backend built with FastAPI for server APIs, session orchestration, model-provider abstraction, safety-policy enforcement, evaluation tooling, dataset processing, and retrieval pipelines.
- A React frontend written in strict TypeScript, using Next.js for the initial web application.
- PostgreSQL as the primary persistence store, with `pgvector` reserved for the approved evidence-retrieval store.
- Redis-compatible infrastructure only for short-lived session coordination, rate limiting, queues, and other ephemeral state; it is not an authoritative safety, consent, entitlement, or transcript store.

The prototype may use free-tier hosting only with synthetic test data, clinician-authored examples, and non-sensitive development accounts. The exact frontend host, backend host, and managed PostgreSQL provider are intentionally deferred until their current terms, security controls, retention, regional storage, backups, logging, access controls, and contracts have been reviewed. No real sensitive conversation, audio, video, or production-pilot data may be placed on a free-tier platform before that review.

## Product and operating constraints adopted with this decision

- Product name: **Glow**.
- Initial pilot: Egypt only; adults aged 18 and above only. The application must not silently provide the adult experience to minors.
- Initial languages: Arabic (with Egypt/Modern Standard Arabic evaluation), English, Spanish, Japanese, and Simplified Chinese. Each requires its own safety, translation, cultural-appropriateness, resource-localization, and harmful-response evaluation before support is claimed.
- Safety positioning: AI mental-health support and care navigation only. Glow must not claim to diagnose, cure, treat, prescribe, independently manage a disorder, replace a professional, or provide emergency services.
- Human escalation: no internal emergency monitoring or intervention is promised for the initial pilot. Crisis help must use verified Egypt-appropriate resources, trusted-person guidance, and professional-care referrals. Crisis-resource access is never blocked by billing, trial, or session limits.
- Data defaults: raw microphone audio, camera video, and avatar-session video are not retained by default. Pilot transcripts are encrypted, retained for 30 days by default, and support immediate user deletion; associated summaries, embeddings, caches, and derived copies must be deleted according to the documented deletion policy. Research and model-improvement use is disabled by default and requires separate, specific, revocable consent if introduced.
- Session policy: standard/paid sessions are 60 minutes with visible timing notices and a structured, non-abrupt closure. Crisis mode overrides ordinary time limits and closure. Any open-conversation limits are server-enforced and never suppress crisis resources or emergency guidance.
- Commercial policy for later billing work: first three completed sessions are free; later 60-minute sessions are priced at USD 14 or EGP 700 when the user selects an available supported currency. The final price, currency, applicable taxes/fees, cancellation, refund policy, and duration must be shown before purchase. This ADR does not select a payment processor or authorize billing implementation.

## Required architectural consequences

- Privileged safety, consent, session-policy, and entitlement decisions are made and revalidated server-side; the client is never authoritative.
- The safety control plane and crisis finite-state machine are independent of the language model and remain testable in isolation. Safety must not exist only in prompts.
- Model, speech, translation, retrieval, and avatar integrations must be behind provider abstractions. Provider selection requires privacy, residency, retention, training-use, licensing, and contractual review.
- The crisis-resource registry is an accountable, periodically verified Egypt-focused locale registry. It must not be generated by a model. Original user wording must be preserved for safety analysis where permitted; translated text alone is insufficient for risk detection.
- The baseline must use strict typing, validated external input, secret management, redacted observability, and no raw therapy content in ordinary logs.

## Consequences

The repository can next be scaffolded as the agreed monorepo, beginning with a minimal health-check endpoint and verified developer commands. The scaffold must not imply that unselected providers, compliance certifications, crisis coverage, human monitoring, or real-data hosting have been approved.

Before a public launch or any acceptance of real sensitive conversations, the team must obtain Egyptian legal, medical, privacy, consumer-protection, and regulatory advice; validate hosting and provider contracts and controls; establish consent, retention, deletion, encryption, access-control, audit, and incident-response mechanisms; and complete clinical safety review for the supported languages and crisis flows.

## Deferred decisions

- Specific frontend, backend, database, authentication, payment, model, speech, translation, avatar, Redis, queue, encryption/KMS, observability, and deployment providers.
- Egypt crisis-resource registry content, verification cadence, accountable operations owner, and legal review of permissible emergency-resource copy.
- Specific database schema, migrations, API contracts, authentication implementation, and production data-residency design.
- Whether and how data-subject deletion propagates through backups after the retention and restoration policy has been legally and operationally approved.
